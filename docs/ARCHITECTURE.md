# Architecture

How the game code is organized and how to extend and test it. Implementation status lives in `PROJECT_STATE.md`; decision rationale lives in `PROJECT_CONTEXT.md`.

Engine: Godot 4.7.2, GDScript with static typing. 1 unit = 1 m; forward is -Z.

`game.gd` also owns a `RenderSettings` object (created after the map loads) that applies the quality profile.

Systems do not search for each other. `game.gd` is the only place that connects them.

## Per-frame data flow

1. **Touch.** The native `VirtualJoystick` writes the `move_left/right/forward/back` actions. `MobileInputController._input()` routes every other touch to one `TouchControl` in priority order (run, punch, roll, interact buttons, the vehicle buttons, `extra_controls`, then look area; hidden controls are skipped) and owns that finger until release. A finger that starts on the look area keeps turning the camera even if it slides over a button. Unclaimed touches fall through to the GUI, where the joystick takes them. A punch or roll button touch-down (or the J / Space key) records a one-shot press that is kept until consumed once (`consume_action_press()`).
2. **Camera (`_process`).** `ThirdPersonCamera` consumes the look delta (radians), clamps pitch, follows the target's interpolated transform, and lets the `SpringArm3D` (sphere, world layer) pull the camera in front of walls.
3. **Player (`_physics_process`).** `PlayerController` clears its `PlayerIntent` and asks its `input_source` to fill it. `TouchPlayerInput` rotates the stick by the camera yaw (`camera_relative`) and copies the pending punch/roll presses into the intent's one-shot fields. The controller puts those presses into its `ActionBuffer`. The active `PlayerState` starts a buffered action or picks the target speed and transitions; `apply_locomotion()` accelerates, turns the body, and applies gravity. Then the buffer ages, `move_and_slide()` runs, and `CharacterVisual.set_locomotion()` gets the horizontal speed.
4. **Vehicle (`_physics_process`).** The driven `VehicleController` asks its `input_source` (`TouchVehicleInput`) for a `VehicleIntent`, turns it into steering, engine force, and per-wheel brakes (`drive_command()`, `steer_angle()`, `approach_steer()`), checks its reset conditions, and passes speed, steering, lights, and wheel poses to its `VehicleVisual`. An intent `exit` press asks `VehicleInteraction` to let the player out. Parked vehicles have no input source and hold the handbrake; once at rest they fall asleep and skip all of this until woken (see "Parked vehicles sleep").
5. **NPCs (`_physics_process`).** Near the player an `Npc` runs the controller of step 3 with `AiPatrolInput` as its input source; farther away a Tween moves it along its route (see "NPCs").
6. **World (`_physics_process`).** `WorldManager` respawns tracked bodies below `kill_height` (NPCs at their own start, the player at the map's spawn) and clamps them to `bounds` (the player is untracked while driving; vehicles reset themselves).

## Extension points

- **New map:** run the import tool on a GLB that follows `docs/MAP_CONTRACT.md` (it writes `maps/<id>/` and the registry entry), or make the scene, `MapDefinition`, and category rules by hand; see "Adding a map". Player, vehicle, NPC, camera, input, profile, and scenario code stay unchanged.
- **New player action:** add a `PlayerState` child under `Player/StateMachine` and a one-shot field on `PlayerIntent`; buffer its press in `PlayerController._physics_process()` and start it from `PlayerState.start_buffered_action()`. States switch with `machine.transition_to(&"Name")`. Punch and roll are the worked examples (see "Character actions").
- **Character model and animations:** replace the `Visual` node of `player.tscn` with a `CharacterVisual` subclass (see "CharacterVisual extension point"). `PlayerController` and the states stay unchanged.
- **Hit targets (NPCs, props):** give the target a `Hurtbox` (physics layer 3) and react to `hit_received(attacker)`. Damage and reactions belong to the target (`Npc` starts its `HitReactionState`).
- **Other drivers (AI, bots, replays):** subclass `PlayerInputSource` and implement `fill_intent()` (`AiPatrolInput` is the worked example). The controller does not know where intents come from.
- **New NPC:** see "Adding an NPC".
- **New vehicle type:** see "Vehicles (Phase 3)", "Adding a vehicle type". A scene plus a `VehicleDefinition`; no code.
- **Other vehicle drivers (AI, replays):** subclass `VehicleInputSource` and set it as the vehicle's `input_source`.
- **New touch control:** subclass `TouchControl`, add it under `HUD/SafeArea/Controls`, and register it in `MobileInputController`'s priority list.

## Physics layers

| Layer | Name | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | world | Map static bodies (GLB `-col` nodes). Mask of the player and the camera spring arm. |
| 2 | player | Player body. |
| 3 | hurtbox | `Hurtbox` areas (training dummy). Mask of the punch `Hitbox`, which is on no layer. |
| 4 | vehicle | Vehicle bodies. Mask: world and vehicle. The player and the camera spring arm mask layers 1 and 4 (value 9), so the player cannot walk through a car and the camera stays out of it. A vehicle's `InteractionArea` masks layer 2 (player). |
| 5 | npc | NPC bodies (Phase 5). Mask: world, player, vehicle, npc (27). The player masks 1, 4, and 5 (25), so the player and NPCs block each other; the camera spring arm keeps 9 and ignores NPCs; vehicles do not mask characters, so a moving car passes through them, as through the player. |

The map has collision on `Ground_*` terrain (streets included), `Buildings_*`, `Mosque_*`, `TreeTrunks_*`, landmarks, and the two bridge decks. Roads, pavements, railings, lamps, decor, and water have no collision of their own. The debug training dummy's body is on layer 1, so the player and the camera spring arm collide with it.

## Input

- Touch is primary. Several fingers work at once; each finger belongs to one control until it lifts.
- Running is a toggle button that turns off when the joystick finger lifts (`run_resets_on_release`).
- Punch and roll buttons fire on touch-down; holding does not repeat, and a drag that starts on them never turns the camera.
- Vehicle controls (shown only while driving): two hold buttons on the left steer (smoothed to an analog value by `steer_rate`), throttle, brake (reverses near standstill), and handbrake "P" on the right, exit at the top right. Getting in uses the on-foot interact button. `set_vehicle_mode()` switches the groups and releases every finger and pending press; punch and roll presses are ignored while driving.
- Desktop development: WASD or arrow keys move (and drive), Shift runs, J punches, Space rolls (handbrake while driving), E gets in and out, and the mouse emulates touch (`emulate_touch_from_mouse`), so left-drag on the left half is the joystick and left-drag on the right half turns the camera.
- When the app loses focus or pauses, `MobileInputController.release_all_touches()` drops routed fingers and pending punch/roll presses, turns the run toggle off, sends touch releases so the native joystick forgets its finger, and releases all input actions (`ACTIONS`, vehicle ones included). `PlayerController` receives the same notifications and ends a running punch or roll (`interrupt_actions()`); a `VehicleController` drops the throttle and holds the handbrake until a pedal is pressed again (`release_controls()`).

## Character actions (Phase 2)

Punch and roll are `PlayerState`s (`PunchState`, `RollState`), not conditions inside the locomotion states.

| Tunable (exported) | Default | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Input buffer window | 0.18 s | `PlayerController.input_buffer_time` |
| Punch startup / active / recovery | 0.10 / 0.10 / 0.25 s | `PunchState` |
| Speed while punching | 0 m/s (stops) | `PunchState.move_speed` |
| Roll duration / distance / cooldown | 0.6 s / 6 m / 0.8 s | `RollState` |
| Horizontal speed kept after a roll | 4.5 m/s | `RollState.exit_speed` |

- **Buffer.** A press is kept for the window (`ActionBuffer`) and dropped afterwards. Locomotion states start a buffered action at the top of their tick: roll first, then punch. Actions start only on the floor (`is_on_floor()`); presses in the air wait in the buffer and expire.
- **Punch.** Startup, then the active phase with the `Hitbox` open (and only then), then recovery; the body decelerates to `move_speed` and keeps its facing. Afterwards a buffered action or the locomotion state matching the stick follows.
- **Roll.** Direction = the intent's movement (camera-relative stick) if any, else the body's facing (`RollState.roll_direction()`); the body turns to it at once. Constant speed `distance / duration`; gravity and `move_and_slide()` keep working, so a roll can leave a bridge deck (the respawn below `kill_height` applies). Roll presses during the roll and its cooldown are ignored (blocked in the buffer), not delayed.
- **Interplay.** A punch pressed during a roll runs when the roll ends if the press is younger than the window. A roll cancels only the punch recovery; a roll pressed during startup or active waits in the buffer and starts on the first recovery tick if it is still young enough. A punch pressed during a punch chains after it the same way.
- **Interruption.** Focus loss or pause: `PlayerController.interrupt_actions()` clears pending presses and switches to Idle; `exit()` closes the hitbox and resets the visual. `teleport()` (spawn, respawn, VIEW) does the same.
- **Hits.** `Hitbox` (Area3D, mask layer 3) hits each `Hurtbox` at most once per activation and calls `Hurtbox.receive_hit(attacker)`, which emits `hit_received`. No damage, knockback, or immunity.
- **Camera.** Unchanged: it follows the player's interpolated transform in `_process`, so the roll's 10 m/s is interpolated like walking (`test_roll_follows_camera_and_camera_follows_roll`).

### CharacterVisual extension point

States ask for visual feedback only through `CharacterVisual` (`game/player/visual/character_visual.gd`), referenced by `PlayerController.visual`:

| Method | Called by | When |
|---|---|---|
| `set_locomotion(speed, on_floor)` | `PlayerController` | every physics tick, after `move_and_slide()` |
| `play_action(action, duration, impact)` | `PunchState.enter()` (`&"punch"`, 0.45 s, impact 0.1 s = start of active), `RollState.enter()` (`&"roll"`, 0.6 s, no impact), `HitReactionState` (`&"hit"`, 0.5 s, NPCs; Phase 5) | action starts |
| `stop_action()` | `PunchState.exit()`, `RollState.exit()`, `HitReactionState.exit()` | action ends, is cancelled, interrupted, or the player teleports |

`PlaceholderCharacterVisual` implements it with tweens on the capsule (lean with speed; the fist thrusts forward on the punch; tuck and one forward turn on the roll). Its tweens run on physics ticks (`Tween.TWEEN_PROCESS_PHYSICS`), so physics interpolation smooths them like the body.

An animated model plugs in as another subclass, for example `AnimatedCharacterVisual` with the model and an `AnimationTree`: `set_locomotion()` sets a locomotion `BlendSpace1D` position from the speed (and a falling state from `on_floor`); `play_action()` fires a `OneShot` or travels an `AnimationNodeStateMachine` to the action, scaling the clip so it lasts `duration` and its contact frame lands at `impact`; `stop_action()` aborts the one-shot and returns to locomotion. Replace the `Visual` node in `player.tscn` with that scene and point `PlayerController.visual` at it; no controller or state code changes. Hit timing stays with `PunchState`, so gameplay does not depend on animation lengths. `test_visual_can_be_replaced` swaps in a recording visual to check this contract.

## Vehicles (Phase 3)

A vehicle is a `VehicleBody3D` scene with `VehicleController` (`game/vehicle/`), following the same intent pattern as the player: a `VehicleInputSource` fills a `VehicleIntent` (`steer` −1..1, `throttle` 0..1, `brake` 0..1, `handbrake`, one-shot `exit`) every physics tick, and the controller never reads raw input. Tunables live in a `VehicleDefinition` resource; meshes live behind `VehicleVisual`.

Physics engine: the project runs **GodotPhysics3D** (the `"DEFAULT"` setting in Godot 4.7.2; `STARTUP … physics_3d=GodotPhysics3D (setting DEFAULT)`). Measured `VehicleBody3D` behaviour on it, which the controller relies on:

- The body drives along **+Z**, so vehicles face +Z (the player faces −Z). `get_follow_yaw()` (`rotation.y + PI`) tells the camera where "behind" is; `place_at(t)` turns the body so that its +Z points along a map transform's −Z (map spawns and viewpoints keep the player's convention).
- Positive `steering` turns **left** with front-wheel steering, so the controller negates the intent's steering (intent +1 = right).
- The project's default linear damping (0.1) capped the top speed near 15 m/s; vehicles replace it with `VehicleDefinition.linear_damp` (0).
- 8° of steering at 21 m/s rolled the car over; the steering angle narrows with speed to `steer_at_max_speed_degrees` (5°), checked by `test_throttle_accelerates_forward_up_to_max_speed`.

| `VehicleDefinition` (compact car) | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `mass`, `center_of_mass`, `linear_damp` | 1200 kg, (0, 0.25, 0), 0 | low centre of mass against rollover |
| `engine_force`, `max_speed` | 3000 N per traction wheel, 22 m/s | force fades linearly to 0 at max speed |
| `reverse_force`, `max_reverse_speed`, `reverse_threshold` | 2000 N, 6 m/s, 1 m/s | brake held below the threshold reverses |
| `brake_force`, `handbrake_force`, `coast_brake`, `hold_speed` | 30, 60 (rear wheels), 1, 0.5 m/s | no pedal: coast; no pedal below `hold_speed`: brakes hold (hills) |
| `max_steer_degrees`, `steer_at_max_speed_degrees`, `steer_rate`, `steer_return_rate` | 30°, 5°, 3/s, 4/s | button steering ramps to an analog value |
| `safe_exit_speed` | 1.5 m/s | getting out allowed at or below |
| `flip_angle_degrees`, `flip_reset_seconds`, `flip_reset_max_speed` | 70°, 2 s, 2 m/s | flipped and nearly still for 2 s → reset |
| `sleep_when_parked`, `sleep_after_seconds`, `sleep_linear_speed`, `sleep_angular_speed` | on, 1 s, 0.1 m/s, 0.1 rad/s | parked at rest for 1 s → asleep (Phase 4b) |
| `camera_profile` | `vehicle_camera_profile.tres` | 7.5 m distance, 1.9 m pivot, follow mode |

- **Drive rules** (`VehicleController.drive_command()`): throttle accelerates (and first brakes a vehicle rolling backwards); brake slows a vehicle moving forward faster than `reverse_threshold` and reverses below it; both pedals brake; the handbrake cuts the engine and brakes the rear wheels. Without an input source (parked) or after focus loss / pause (`release_controls()`), the handbrake holds until a pedal is pressed.
- **Getting in** (`VehicleInteraction`): the vehicle's `InteractionArea` tracks the player; the interact button shows while a vehicle is in reach and the player stands on a floor. Entering calls `PlayerController.set_seated(true)` (hidden, collision shape disabled, no physics processing, actions and presses dropped), keeps the player at the `Seat` marker every tick, switches `MobileInputController` to vehicle mode, gives the vehicle `TouchVehicleInput`, and calls `ThirdPersonCamera.set_target(vehicle, definition.camera_profile)`. `game.gd` untracks the player in `WorldManager` meanwhile.
- **Getting out** (`request_exit()`): refused at speeds above `safe_exit_speed` ("speed"; the exit button is faded meanwhile). Otherwise `find_exit_transform()` tries each exit marker in order (driver's side first): a ray finds the floor under it, and a shape query with the player's capsule must find no world or vehicle collider there; if no marker qualifies the exit is refused ("blocked"). Refused presses are dropped. On success the vehicle is parked (handbrake), the player is placed and unseated, and the on-foot controls and camera profile return.
- **Reset:** below the map's `kill_height`, or flipped as above, the vehicle returns to its spawn (`spawn_transform`), and a seated driver goes with it.
- **Parked vehicles sleep** (Phase 4b, P2): see below.
- **Camera follow mode:** `CameraProfile.follow_yaw_sharpness` > 0 swings the yaw back behind the target after `follow_delay` seconds without look input (vehicle profile: 2.5, 1 s); the on-foot profile keeps 0 (no change from Phase 1). The spring arm masks the world and vehicle layers and excludes the followed body.
- **Placement:** `MapDefinition.vehicle_spawns` (transforms facing −Z); `WorldManager.spawn_vehicles(Main.vehicle_scene)` parks one vehicle per entry; `game.gd` registers them with `VehicleInteraction`. VIEW moves the vehicle instead of the player while driving.
- **HUD cost** (Mac, low profile, same view): HUD hidden 67 draw calls; on-foot HUD 105 (+5 while the interact button shows); vehicle HUD 118.

### Parked vehicles sleep (Phase 4b)

A `VehicleBody3D` at rest is never put to sleep by GodotPhysics3D: the engine's own sleep test marks it inactive at the end of a step, and its wheel raycasts apply suspension impulses in the next state callback, which wake it again. So it is simulated every step, and between steps (where the overlay and the benchmark look) it reads as inactive and `RigidBody3D.sleeping` as `true`. Phase 4a measured ≈ 1 ms per physics step on the Galaxy Tab A7 for the unused parked car. `VehicleController` therefore puts it to sleep itself:

| Rule | Where | Value (compact car) |
|---|---|---|
| Sleeps when: no driver (`input_source` null), all wheels in contact, linear speed < `sleep_linear_speed`, angular speed < `sleep_angular_speed`, for `sleep_after_seconds` | `_update_rest()`; `VehicleDefinition`, group "Parking" (`sleep_when_parked` switches it off) | 0.1 m/s, 0.1 rad/s, 1.0 s |
| Sleeping = `RigidBody3D.sleeping = true`: the physics server stops simulating the body (no wheel raycasts, suspension, or solver work); velocities are zeroed; brake values stay set | | |
| State: `is_asleep()` is true while the parking rule put the vehicle to sleep and the physics server still has it inactive (`BODY_STATE_SLEEPING`); neither the `sleeping` property nor the server state alone is reliable (above). `sleep_changed(asleep)` fires at once for the rule and `wake_up()`, and on the next tick for a wake by the engine | | |
| Wakes when a driver gets in (the `input_source` setter) | `wake_up()` | |
| Wakes when moved or reset: `place_at()` (spawn, VIEW, teleports), `reset_to_spawn()` | `wake_up()` (setting the velocities there already wakes the body in GodotPhysics3D; the call keeps the rule independent of that) | |
| Wakes when a rigid body hits it | the physics engine (sleeping rigid bodies join the island of the body that touches them) | |
| Wakes when the player walks into it (kinematic bodies do not wake it by themselves) | `PlayerController._wake_bodies_touched()`, only while `is_on_wall()` and the intent moves (Phase 4c: standing still against a car on a slope keeps a wall contact and kept it awake); calls `wake_up()` on colliders that have it | |
| Wakes when the ground under a wheel is gone (floor removed or switched off) | `_check_ground()`: while asleep, one ray per wheel along the body's down axis every `GROUND_CHECK_INTERVAL` (0.25 s), length radius + rest length + 0.15 m | |

- While asleep, `_physics_process()` only checks the ground; engine, brake, and steering values and the visual stay as they were, so the parked handbrake and hold brakes are still set when it wakes. On a slope it does not move while asleep, and after waking the usual rules apply (parked: handbrake; driver without pedals: hill hold). The `InteractionArea` is a separate `Area3D` and keeps detecting the player while the vehicle sleeps.
- The player cannot push a vehicle (vehicles do not mask the player layer, unchanged from Phase 3), so a player bump only wakes it; it falls asleep again 1 s after the contact ends.
- **Why sleep, not freeze:** measured headless on the real map (`tools/perf/vehicle_sleep_cost.gd`, `docs/perf/phase4b/headless/vehicle_sleep_cost.txt`): both take the body out of the server's active list (0 active bodies) and cost the same as removing the vehicle from the simulation within noise. A sleeping body stays a rigid body that the engine wakes on contact (a thrown 400 kg box woke it and pushed it 0.38 m), while a frozen (static) body stays an immovable wall that does not wake (the same box moved it 0.000 m) and every wake case would need manual handling.
- **Not `can_sleep = false`:** switching the engine's own sleep test off would make the server state reliable between steps, but then any active body sharing the vehicle's island (the player standing against it, a box resting on it) wakes it after every step, so it never stays asleep (tried in Phase 4b; `test_walking_into_a_sleeping_vehicle_wakes_it` and `test_a_body_hitting_a_sleeping_vehicle_wakes_it` failed).
- Debug overlay: `car asleep|awake` after the player's (or, while driving, the vehicle's) speed; `PERF … vehicles=asleep`; `VEHICLE <name> asleep|awake` log line on each change (debug and Release Perf builds); `STARTUP … vehicles=…` and `startup.vehicles`; benchmark results record `vehicles_asleep` (share of frames).

## Maps and the map registry (Phase 5)

- **Registry:** `game/world/maps.tres` (`MapRegistry.PATH`) lists the loadable `MapDefinition`s; `Main._ready()` takes it through `MapRegistry.for_build()` in debug and Release Perf builds (`main.tscn` names no registry since Phase 6d; tests and tools set `Main.maps` themselves), in this order: the shader facade map (`istanbul_halic_cells_shader`, the default since 2026-11-02, D25), İstanbul • Haliç (`istanbul_halic`), the test district, the cells map (`istanbul_halic_cells`), the light facade map (`istanbul_halic_cells_light`). The default is the first *available* entry (`MapRegistry.default_map()`, below), and it is the only map a normal release build loads: those builds take `game/world/maps_release.tres` (`MapRegistry.RELEASE_PATH`), which lists only the maps the release presets pack (today the default map; "Release builds"). Debug and Release Perf builds start on another map with an experiment's `"map"` key or `--map=<id>` (in that order; an unknown or unavailable id falls back to the default with a warning, an unknown experiment map is rejected by `PerfConfig`); debug builds load the next map with the overlay's MAP button. Tests set `Main.requested_map` before the scene enters the tree; the İstanbul tests (`test_game_on_map.gd`) request `istanbul_halic`.
- **Facade maps** (D24, D25): `istanbul_halic_cells_shader` (first in the registry, the default) and `istanbul_halic_cells_light` (experimental, after the cells map) are the cells map's data on the GLBs of `--facade light` / `--facade shader`, sharing the cells map's occluders (their collision is the same). The shader map's script (`istanbul_halic_cells_shader_map.gd`, extending İstanbul's) replaces every surface whose GLB material is `Istanbul_FacadeWindows` with a `ShaderMaterial` on `facade_windows.gdshader` (`set_surface_override_material`); the shader reads `UV`, `UV2`, and `COLOR` and works with the Mobile renderer. Since 2026-11-01 it box-filters every element over the pixel's footprint on the wall (`fwidth(UV)`; the exact share of the pixel each element covers, from the integral of its periodic pattern; uniform `filter_pixels`, 1) instead of fading to an average colour beyond 0.35–0.9 m of wall per pixel: windows keep their contrast while a pixel or more wide (`docs/perf/facade_v2/`). One shader for every profile. The script also reads the measurement-only runtime keys `facade_material`, `facade_priority`, and `facade_warmup` (`apply_runtime_settings()`, called by `Main` for a map that has it; "Performance experiments"); `facade_windows_simple.gdshader` exists only for `facade_material: simple`. Tests: `tests/integration/facade_map_case.gd` (shared) and one `test_istanbul_halic_cells_<variant>.gd` per map. Measurement experiments: `profile_default_cells_<variant>`, `…_pss`, `c_cells_<variant>_low_vis150_nofog`, `session_profile_cells_<variant>`; device scenarios on the shader map: `scenario_vehicle_sleep_cells_shader`, `scenario_extensibility_cells_shader`; the tablet's stutter A/B (2026-11-01): `c_stutter_{shader,flat,simple,warmup,priority,cells}` and `c_stutter_cap31` (a 31 FPS cap), all traced; the relative criterion of D25 (2026-11-02): `c_relative_shader` and `c_relative_istanbul`. The user chose the shader facade (D25); it is the default since 2026-11-02.
- **Availability:** a map whose files are generated or copied in on each machine lists them in `MapDefinition.required_files` (each İstanbul map: its GLB; the test district needs none). `has_required_files()` checks that each can be loaded: for an imported file, that the import result named in its `.import` file exists (exported builds carry only the result; a checkout has the committed `.import` before the GLB is generated and imported). `is_available()` is true when they can, or else when the map's streaming index can be used (`MapStreamingIndex.usable()`: the resident scene and every cell present, the model unchanged where it exists; Phase 6d), so a package that carries a map only as its cells, without its GLB, can load it (`docs/STREAMING.md`, "Backward compatibility"). Code that needs the whole scene (the cell step's `--all`, comparisons with the map loaded whole) asks `has_required_files()`. `MapRegistry.default_map()` is the first available map, null when none is (T44): a fresh clone without the shader map's GLB starts on İstanbul • Haliç if its GLB is copied in, else on the test district, with the warning `Default map 'istanbul_halic_cells_shader' is not available (missing: …); loading '…'`, in every kind of build. The MAP button skips unavailable maps (`MapRegistry.next_after()`), and `--map` or an experiment naming one falls back to the default with a warning (`Main._initial_map()`). With no available map, `Main._ready()` logs an error naming each map's missing files and shows "Harita yüklenemedi: harita dosyaları eksik." on the HUD's loading screen (`UIController.show_fatal_error()`) instead of loading.
- **Switching:** `Main.switch_map(definition)` is a coroutine; it is refused while driving and while another switch is running (`Main.switching`). Otherwise the HUD shows its loading screen ("Yükleniyor...", `UIController.set_loading()`: controls hidden and stopped, every finger released), the player's processing stops (so it does not fall without a map), the training dummy is removed, the old map's `RenderSettings` goes back to the scene defaults (so the new map captures those, not the profile's values), and `WorldManager.unload_map_and_wait()` frees the old map with its vehicles and NPCs (since Phase 6e its crowded nodes first, 512 per frame, after its streamer stops) and waits until they are gone (`queue_free()` frees at the end of the frame) plus `RELEASE_FRAMES` (3) frames, in which the rendering device releases their meshes, then emits `map_unloaded`. Only then is the new map loaded: with a RenderingDevice behind the loading screen, read and instantiated on threads and set up in steps over frames (`Main._load_behind_screen()`, `docs/STREAMING.md`, "Loading a map"; Phase 6e), headless or with `"threaded_load": false` at once (`_load_map()`); either way the player goes to the new spawn, the camera snaps behind it, the vehicles and NPCs are placed and registered, and a new `RenderSettings` is created; the experiment's runtime keys and the quality profile are applied again, the loading screen goes, and it logs `MAP <id> loaded in <ms> ms (<old id> freed after <ms> ms, <n> frames)` and emits `map_changed`. So two maps are never in memory at once (until 2026-11-01 the new map was loaded while the old one was only queued for freeing, and the 3 GB tablet's low-memory killer stopped the app twice). The node count after a round trip equals the count before it, and the old map's scene is out of the resource cache when `map_unloaded` fires (tests on the test district, İstanbul, and the cells and facade maps; the device scenarios).
- **Per-map data** (`MapDefinition`): `id`, `display_name`, `scene_path`, player spawn and yaw, `bounds`, `kill_height`, `vehicle_spawns` with an optional `vehicle_scenes` entry per spawn (`Main.vehicle_scene` where empty), `npc_routes` (`PatrolRoute`: points and a wait; one NPC per route), `occluders_path`, `required_files`, debug viewpoints and names.
- **Map categories** (2026-11-02, Phase 6a): `MapDefinition.category_rules` (`CategoryRules`, `game/world/category_rules.gd`) says which render category (`CategoryRules.CATEGORIES`: facade_detail, decor, lamps, trees, buildings, ground, water) each mesh belongs to, which categories' collision becomes occluders (`occluder_categories`, buildings and ground), and the category of meshes no rule matches (`default_category`, none by default). Its `rules` (`CategoryRule`) are tried in order, the first match wins: `prefix` (node name starts with), `regex` (found in the node name), `words` (a word of the node name starts with a listed word; `CategoryRules.words_of()` splits at non-letters and lower-to-upper case changes), `material` (regex on a surface material's name), `group` (a Godot group). `RenderSettings` takes the rules from `Main._load_map()` and holds no map's names (`test_render_settings_has_no_map_specific_names`). The four İstanbul maps share `maps/istanbul_halic/istanbul_halic_categories.tres` (the 29 prefixes RenderSettings had built in), the test district has its own; `test_map_categories_unchanged.gd` proves every mesh of the five maps kept its category (against the old table, `tests/fixtures/legacy_categories.gd`).
- **Streaming** (Phase 6c, `docs/STREAMING.md`): a definition's `streaming_index_path` names a `MapStreamingIndex` written by `tools/cell_map.gd`; when it can be used (`MapStreamingIndex.load_for()`: present, complete, the model unchanged), `WorldManager.load_map()` loads its resident scene (the map scene with the facade detail, tree, lamp, and decor meshes taken out of their nodes) and a `MapStreamer` that gives those nodes their meshes from `MapCell` files near the camera; otherwise the map loads whole, as before. `Main` sets the streamer's focus (the camera), its ranges (after every `set_quality_profile()`), and primes it after a load (`_prime_streaming()`, before the first frame or before the loading screen goes); `unload_map()` shuts it down. A map script may implement `prepare_streamed(instances)` for meshes that arrive later (the İstanbul maps' vertex colours, the shader facade) and `prepare_streamed_materials(materials)`, called once at load with the streamed meshes' materials (a material changed at its first use made the tablet build a shader in that frame). The streamer loads the index's warm cells (the smallest cell per material) with the map and keeps them, so every material is in the scene from the start; one threaded request is in flight at a time. The test district and the Apollo relief have nothing to stream.
- **The cells map** (`maps/istanbul_halic_cells/`): the İstanbul scene structure (`Model`, `WorldEnvironment`, `Sun`, the same map script `istanbul_halic_map.gd`) on `assets/istanbul_halic_cells.glb`, and a copy of İstanbul's `MapDefinition` data with its own id, scene, occluders (`istanbul_halic_cells_occluders.scn`, baked from its collision), and `required_files`. Generating its GLB: `docs/maps/istanbul_halic/README.md`, "The cells map". Measured with `profile_default_cells.json` (memory: `profile_default_cells_pss.json`) beside `profile_default.json`, and `c_cells_low_vis150_nofog.json` (the low profile with 150 m ranges and no fog, an experiment only).

### Adding a map

Since Phase 6a (2026-11-02) a map is added with the import tool; the map contract says what a GLB must and should provide (`docs/MAP_CONTRACT.md`, which also has the user's steps):

1. Put the GLB (or a `.gltf` with its files) anywhere on disk (it is read, never copied or changed); convert OBJ, STL, or FBX first with `tools/convert_to_gltf.gd -- --out=<file.glb> <input>…`. Optionally write a configuration JSON (scale, up axis, category rules, collider types, fixed points); the tool finds a Z-up or upside-down file and millimetres, centimetres, inches, or feet by itself.
2. Run `godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/import_map.gd -- --glb=<file> [--config=<file>] [--id=<id>]` (exit 0 written, 1 a mandatory rule failed, 2 bad arguments).
3. Read `docs/maps/imported/<id>.md`: every FAIL says what to change in the model or the configuration, and "How to improve this map" what the tool had to guess or fix; run the command again (`--compare=<previous report>` shows both runs side by side).
4. The map is in the registry (after the existing maps; the default does not change): press MAP in a debug build, or start with `--map=<id>` (debug and Release Perf builds).
5. Streaming: the tool has written `maps/<id>/streaming/` (the cell step; `--no-cells` skips it) and pointed the definition at it; on another machine run `tools/cell_map.gd -- --map=<id>` (or `--all`) and `--import` (`docs/STREAMING.md`).
6. Test it: a file in `tests/integration/` that extends `imported_map_case.gd` with the definition path and the expected categories runs the spawn, the 10 s walk, punch and roll, the vehicle, the NPC, the profiles, a map switch, and the device scenario on it (`test_imported_kenney_town.gd` is the model; it also walks the player at a tree and a fence). Shots for the report: `tools/capture_map_shots.gd -- --definition=res://maps/<id>/<id>.tres --out=<dir>`.

What the game reads is the same for an imported map and a hand-made one: `maps/<id>/<id>.tscn` (a `Model`, a `WorldEnvironment`, a `Sun`), the `MapDefinition` (`maps/<id>/<id>.tres`: spawn, bounds, kill height, vehicle spawns and types, NPC routes, occluders, category rules, required files, viewpoints, the sloped viewpoint), and an entry in `game/world/maps.tres`. Player, vehicle, NPC, camera, input, profile, and scenario code stay unchanged.

**A map by hand** (as the test district, `tools/build_test_district.gd`): any Node3D scene with walkable static bodies on layer 1 under its meshes, a `CategoryRules` resource for its mesh names, optional baked occluders (`tools/bake_occluders.gd -- --map=res://maps/<id>/<id>.tres`, from trimesh, convex, and box collision), a hand-written `MapDefinition` (typed arrays, not packed arrays, T16; NPC routes at least 10 m from where tests walk), and the registry entry.

### Import tool (Phase 6a, 2026-11-02; hardened in Phase 6b, 2026-11-03)

`tools/import_map.gd` turns a glTF map into a game map (rules and user steps: `docs/MAP_CONTRACT.md`; one command, the file is only read). Other formats go through `tools/convert_to_gltf.gd` first (OBJ with MTL or a same-named texture, binary or ASCII STL, FBX through Godot's `FBXDocument`; geometry written as it is, no unit or axis change). The pipeline lives in `tools/map_import/` (no `class_name`; tests preload the scripts):

| Script | Does |
|---|---|
| `map_importer.gd` | Loads the file with `GLTFDocument` (C1); flattens every mesh into one `Model` node with rigid transforms, baking the scale (configured, or a common unit found by `_detect_unit()`) and any scale or mirroring of the source nodes into the meshes (`_snapped` keys share one bake per scale); turns closed inside-out meshes outward (`_fix_inside_out()`, C7); finds the up direction (`_detect_up()`, C3) and turns the nodes rigidly when it is not +Y; flat normals where the source has none (R4); vertex colours on white untextured materials, optional back-face culling (R5, R6); the checks C2, C3, C5, C6 (`_audit_scale_and_axes`); categories (configured rules or a `CategoryRules` resource, then the vocabulary; a mesh no name matched that holds several grounded objects is split into one mesh per part (`multi_parts()`, `_split()`, R3); then shape, with `MapGeometry.profile()` and repeat counts, and a second look at meshes on buildings (`_attachments()`: facade detail, roofs); shape results become Godot groups `map_category_<category>` so the saved rules reproduce them; R1, R3, R7); open building shells (C7); colliders (R2); occluders (`build_occluders()`, the same `RenderSettings.build_occluders` the bake uses). Every check is a `{"id", "result", "message"}` in `checks`. |
| `map_units.gd` | Pure functions of measurements: `detect_up()` (flattest axis and flat faces, then the base end, the winding, or the heights decide the side), `unit_of()` (mm, cm, inch, foot when exactly one gives a map-sized extent and 2–40 m objects), `suggest_scale()`, `nice()`, the rotations to +Y. |
| `map_geometry.gd` | Face statistics (area facing each axis direction with area-weighted coordinates, vertical area, upward cover, normal sum, volume, zero-area and flipped triangles), flat normals, baked transforms (mirroring reverses the winding), turning a mesh inside out, connected parts as point lists or as meshes of their own (`part_meshes()`), the vertex profile over the height (trunk and crown), closed-shell check. Godot's front faces wind clockwise: front normal `(c − a) × (b − a)`. |
| `map_classifier.gd` | The default vocabulary (as `words` rules), the group rules, configured rules, the shape rules (`category_by_shape()`), the shape key for repeats. |
| `map_colliders.gd` | Collision node suffixes (`-col`, `-colonly`, `-convcol`, `-convcolonly`) and the collider types: `auto` by category and shape (ground trimesh; building parts their hull, or their own triangles when concave, `is_concave()`; trees and lamps an upright capsule round the trunk; decor parts a capsule, a box, or their own triangles; nothing under 0.5 m or 0.3 m high), `trimesh`, `convex` and `box` per connected part, `capsule`. A `StaticBody3D` (layer 1) under the mesh, as Godot's GLB import makes it. |
| `map_placement.gd` | C4 and C8 with rays and shape queries against the generated colliders (the model in the tree): walkable samples on a grid (extent / 100, 2–10 m), the player spawn and the vehicle place scored over up to 150 candidates at least 6 m apart (walk or drive, buildings beside it, room, trees, lamps, and decor nearby, distance, slope), the NPC route, three viewpoints facing the centre, a sloped viewpoint, the bounds (walkable extent, 1 m inside), the kill height (10 m below the lowest geometry); points from the configuration replace the search. |
| `map_writer.gd` | The map scene (text: `Model` instance, the İstanbul sky and sun), the `MapDefinition` (saved with `ResourceSaver`), the registry entry (appended to `game/world/maps.tres` as text, so the other entries keep their ids). |
| `import_report.gd` | The Markdown report: checks, "How to improve this map" (`ADVICE` per rule), the comparison with earlier reports (`--compare`, `read_report()`), categories, colliders, placement, timings, files. |
| `sample_sheet.gd` | Evaluation: contact sheets of random meshes of a map for hand-labelling (`--isolate` for hidden ones) and a TSV with the tool's categories. |
| `compare_categories.gd` | Evaluation: two imports of the same map compared mesh by mesh (a shape-only import against the names), as a confusion table. |

Outputs (`--out`, default `maps/<id>/`): `<id>_model.scn` (compressed; above 10 MB the tool writes a `.gitignore` for it), `<id>_categories.tres`, `<id>_occluders.scn`, `<id>.tscn`, `<id>.tres`, `streaming/` (the cell step of `tools/cell_map.gd`, `tools/map_import/map_cells.gd`; not with `--no-cells`), and the registry entry (not with `--no-register`); the report goes to `docs/maps/imported/<id>.md` (`--report`). Nothing is written while a mandatory check fails (`--force` writes anyway); exit code 0 written, 1 not written, 2 bad arguments. `tools/capture_map_shots.gd` takes the report's four shots (spawn, vehicle, the map from above with posts at the spawn, vehicle, and route, the NPC) of any map definition, or one view of a raw glTF (`--glb`). Tests: `tests/unit/test_map_import.gd`, `test_map_colliders.gd`, `test_map_placement.gd`, `test_convert_to_gltf.gd` (synthetic scenes), and the imported maps' gameplay tests (`tests/integration/imported_map_case.gd` and a file per map).

### Map generation (İstanbul • Haliç)

The İstanbul • Haliç GLB is generated outside Godot by a Python script (T39). It reads `maps/istanbul_halic/source/` and writes to `build/maps/istanbul_halic/` (ignored by Git); `maps/istanbul_halic/reference/` holds the records of the shipped GLB for comparison. Setup, output files, and the Blender preview script: `docs/maps/istanbul_halic/README.md`, "Regenerating the map".

```bash
python3.12 -m venv <VENV> && <VENV>/bin/python -m pip install -r tools/maps/istanbul_halic/requirements.txt
<VENV>/bin/python -B tools/maps/istanbul_halic/build_map.py        # --source DIR, --out DIR
<VENV>/bin/python -B tools/maps/istanbul_halic/compare_outputs.py  # vs assets/istanbul_halic.glb and reference/
```

- Structure of `build_map.py`: module-level stages in order (DEM sampling, terrain, quays, streets, building shells and facade detail, landmarks via `architecture.py`, Galata and Atatürk bridges, the metro bridge via `bridge.py`, trees and gardens via `vegetation.py`, lamps, signals, and road paint via `street_details.py`, ferries), each adding faces to named meshes; `export_glb()` writes them. The modules get the generator's globals (`g['mesh']`, `g['chunk']`, `g['OUT']`, …).
- Mesh names are the contract with the game: `<Category>_<cx>_<cy>` for 150 m chunks (the prefixes of `maps/istanbul_halic/istanbul_halic_categories.tres`), `<Category>_<cx>_<cy>_<i>_<j>` for the cells of `--detail-cell` (`Detail_`, `Trees_`, `Shrubs_` only), a `-col` suffix for static collision (827 bodies), per-landmark names for mosques and bridges.
- `--detail-cell METRES` and `compare_outputs.py --grouped` (cell grouping and its proof): `docs/maps/istanbul_halic/README.md`, "Cell grouping". The cells map uses 40 m (`PROJECT_STATE.md`, "Cell grouping and the cells map").
- `--facade full|light|shader` and `compare_outputs.py --facade` (lighter facade detail, only `Detail_*` changes): README, "Facade variants". `shader` writes `TEXCOORD_0` / `TEXCOORD_1` and a seventh material on the facade meshes; meshes without UVs are written exactly as before.
- Seeds are fixed (`random.Random(41023)`, vegetation `4102303`), so a run is repeatable; GEOS (bundled with shapely) decides road, pavement, and quay triangulation, which is why the shapely version is pinned.
- A regenerated GLB is never copied over `assets/istanbul_halic.glb` by the tools; İstanbul • Haliç, its tests, and its baked occluders keep using the shipped one. The cells map's GLB is copied to `assets/istanbul_halic_cells.glb` by hand, as documented.

|---|---|---|
| `Npc` (`extends PlayerController`) | Sets `AiPatrolInput` as its input source, counts hits (`hits`, `hit` signal), starts the hit reaction; `patrol(route)` places it on the route's first point facing the second; switches the far mode by the distance to `focus` (the player, set by `Main`) every 0.25 s | `far_distance` 10 m, `near_distance` 8 m |
| `AiPatrolInput` (`PlayerInputSource`) | Walks toward the current point (stick tilt `move_scale` → half the walk speed), waits `wait_seconds` there, loops; gives a point up after `stuck_seconds` of walking without getting `min_progress` closer (signals `arrived`, `gave_up`) | 0.5, arrive 0.6 m, 2 s, 0.25 m |
| `HitReactionState` (`PlayerState`) | Pushed away from the attacker at `push_speed`, slows to a stop, keeps its facing for `duration`; a second hit restarts it; then a buffered action or the locomotion state | 0.5 s, 2 m/s |
| `PatrolRoute` (`Resource`, in `MapDefinition.npc_routes`) | Points (x, z steer; y is the placement height) and the wait at each | 1.5 s |
| `PlaceholderCharacterVisual` | `play_action(&"hit", …)`: rocks back and squashes, then straightens | — |

- The one-hit-per-punch rule is the `Hitbox`'s (T21) and holds for NPCs: a punch that stays in reach hits once.
- Physics: NPCs and the player block each other (the player's mask now includes layer 5); the camera's spring arm ignores NPCs; vehicles do not collide with NPCs (as with the player, T24: a vehicle passes through a standing character, the character cannot walk through a vehicle). An NPC walking into a parked car wakes it (the `PlayerController` rule).
- Placement: an NPC gets its start transform before it enters the tree (`Npc.start_transform()`): GodotPhysics3D moves a character body that is already in the space to a new transform only in the next physics step, so a body spawned at the origin and then teleported would, for that one step, push whatever stands at the origin (found in a unit test: an NPC ended up on the player's head and rode along).
- **Far mode (physics level of detail):** farther than `far_distance` from the player (back within `near_distance`) an NPC leaves the controller: its `_physics_process` only checks the distance, and a Tween (physics process mode, so physics interpolation smooths it) moves it to the patrol input's current point at the speed the controller would walk, through floor heights sampled with one ray every 2 m when the segment starts; at the point it calls `AiPatrolInput.reach_point()`, waits the route's wait, and walks on. Near the player it is the controller again, from where it stands, toward the same point. Measured on the Galaxy Tab A7 (`PROJECT_STATE.md`, "Phase 5"): two walking NPCs under the controller added 2.3–2.7 ms to every physics step (three-run A/B; on the Mac a character costs about the same on boxes as on trimesh, so this is mostly script and transform work); a far walk scripted every tick still cost about 0.6–0.75 ms per NPC; the Tween walk adds 0.55–0.64 ms for the two. Far NPCs pass through walls, cars, and each other, so routes must be clear; a hit reaction always plays out under the controller.
- Measurement: the `"npcs"` runtime key (false) takes the NPCs out of the simulation and `"npc_far_mode"` (false) keeps them under the controller at any distance; results record `npcs` (NPCs simulated) and `npcs_near` (average number under the controller); `tools/perf/npc_cost.gd` measures the physics step headless with the NPCs off, as in the game, and all under the controller.

## Safe area and screen layouts (Phase 4c)

- **Source:** `UIController.safe_area_source` (`SafeAreaSource`, `game/ui/safe_area_source.gd`) gives the window size and the display safe area in window pixels. The default asks `DisplayServer` (`get_display_safe_area()` on mobile; on desktop there is none and the whole window is safe), so devices behave as before. `SafeAreaSource.fixed(window, safe)` stands in for any screen in tests and in the screenshot tool.
- **Applying:** `apply_safe_area()` converts the safe area to canvas units (`safe_rect_in_viewport()`; `UIController.safe_rect` keeps the result) and insets `SafeArea` by it plus `edge_margin` (16 units). It runs at start, on viewport size changes, and when the HUD's check every `SAFE_AREA_CHECK_INTERVAL` (0.25 s) sees a different window size or safe area: a 180° turn between the two landscape orientations moves a punch-hole or a navigation bar to the other side without a size change, which the viewport never reports. Any change after the first layout calls `MobileInputController.release_all_touches()`, as on focus loss, so a held joystick or button cannot stay down on a control that moved. Debug and Release Perf builds log `SAFE_AREA window=… safe=… canvas=… viewport=…` on every application. Notched iPhones report symmetric side insets in landscape (47 pt each on the iPhone 13 Pro, per Apple's layout guides; the device's own line was not captured in Phase 4c), so a turn changes nothing there.
- **Layout matrix** (`tests/unit/test_hud_layout.gd`): nine screens with their safe areas in window pixels (`SCREENS`): 16:9 1920×1080; 19.5:9 iPhone 13 Pro 2532×1170 with the notch on the left and on the right; 20:9 2400×1080 with a punch-hole (110 px) on either side and a 3-button bar (126 px) on the other side or a gesture handle (63 px) at the bottom; 4:3 iPad 2048×1536; 16:10 Tab A7 2000×1200. For each, on foot (with the interact button) and in the vehicle, with the debug overlay and buttons shown, it checks: every visible button inside the platform safe area; hit circles (drawn radius × `hit_scale`) apart; the right-side buttons entirely over the camera drag area and the steering buttons entirely left of it; the joystick zone left of the camera drag area and the joystick at rest (its `initial_offset_ratio`, radius `joystick_size / 2`) inside the safe area and clear of every button; the debug label clear of every button; every hit target and the joystick at least 7 mm on the device (`MIN_TARGET_MM`; Apple's 44 pt minimum is ~7 mm on an iPhone). Size on the device = hit diameter in canvas units × window pixels per canvas unit (the root's final transform: `min(window.x / 1440, window.y / 900)` with `expand`) ÷ the screen's pixels per inch × 25.4. Insets of the Android phones and the ppi values are assumed typical values, not measured devices. The test prints one `layout …` line per screen and group (canvas size, scale, smallest target and gap); results: `PROJECT_STATE.md`, "Phase 4c".
- **Adding a screen:** add an entry to `SCREENS` (window size, safe area in window pixels as the platform reports it, ppi) and a `test_<name>()` that calls `_check_screen()`; take a reference shot with `tools/capture_screenshot.gd --safe-area=…` (below) at a smaller resolution with the same aspect ratio, scaling the safe area by the same factor. Reference shots: `docs/ui/`.

## Quality profiles

Permanent render settings per device class, applied in every build.

| Profile | Default on | Settings (unset keys keep the scene default) |
|---|---|---|
| none | desktop, editor, tests | none |
| high | iOS | 60 FPS cap, occlusion culling, 200 m visibility range for trees, lamps, decor, facade detail |
| low | Android | 30 FPS cap, occlusion culling, 100 m visibility ranges (same categories), 400 m far plane with depth fog, shadows off, 0.6 render scale, LOD threshold 4 |

- **Resources:** `QualityProfile` (`game/quality/quality_profile.gd`), `game/quality/profiles/{high,low}.tres`. `to_settings()` returns only the keys the profile sets.
- **Selection** (`QualityProfiles`, strongest first): a debug experiment's `profile` key → `--quality=<none|high|low>` on the command line → platform rule (`OS.has_feature("ios")` → high, `"android"` → low, otherwise none). No GPU detection.
- **Applying:** `RenderSettings` (`game/quality/render_settings.gd`) applies a settings dictionary over the defaults it captured from the scene: map-category visibility and visibility ranges (categories from the map's `CategoryRules`), camera far plane, depth fog on the loaded environment (fading to the map's procedural sky horizon colour, else its background colour), sun shadow mode, viewport scale/LOD/occlusion, `Engine.max_fps`. Keys a layer leaves out are reset to the defaults, so `set_quality_profile()` can switch at runtime.
- **Precedence:** experiment > profile > defaults (`RenderSettings.merge`, per key; dictionary keys such as `visibility_end` are replaced as a whole).
- **Occluders:** with occlusion on, `RenderSettings` loads `MapDefinition.occluders_path` (baked); if it is missing it builds occluders from the `buildings` and `ground` collision and logs a warning.

## Performance experiments (debug and Release Perf builds)

Temporary settings layered on top of a profile for measurements. They run in debug builds and in release builds exported with the `perf_bench` custom feature (see "Build features"); normal release builds ignore them. With no experiment and the "none" profile the game runs with scene defaults (`test_no_perf_experiment_by_default`).

- **Flags:** `PerfConfig` (`game/debug/perf_config.gd`) holds one experiment, parsed from JSON; unknown keys, categories, or profiles are rejected. Keys: `experiment`, `profile` (`none`/`high`/`low`; omitted = platform default), `map` (a registry id; omitted = the default map; Phase 5; since the shader map became the default on 2026-11-02, every experiment measured before then pins `istanbul_halic` and only the `scenario_*` files leave it out, so the device scenarios start on the default), the `RenderSettings` keys `hide` (categories), `visibility_end` ({category: metres}), `camera_far` + `fog`, `shadows` (`default`/`off`/`single`), `render_scale` + `scaling_mode` (`bilinear`/`metalfx`; FSR is Forward+ only in Godot 4.7.2), `max_fps`, `occlusion`, `lod_threshold`, a `runtime` section (below), and an optional `bench` section (`warmup_s` 30, `measure_s` 60, `views`, `session_minutes`, `session_view`, `screenshots`, `quit_when_done`, `in_vehicle`, `trace`), or a `scenario` section (debug builds; see "Device scenario"). Experiments from the 2026-10-24 diagnosis pin `"profile": "none"`, the condition they were measured under; Phase 4 experiments (`c_*`, `pacing_*`, `jolt_*`, `session_pacing_*`) name the device profile (`low` tablet, `high` iPhone).
- **Runtime keys** (`PerfConfig.RUNTIME_KEYS`, applied by `Main.apply_runtime_settings()`, measurement only): `physics_ticks` (Engine physics rate), `physics_interpolation`, `player_physics` (false pauses the player's `_physics_process`, so no `move_and_slide`), `vehicles` (false takes the parked vehicles out of the simulation), `collider_radius` (map static bodies whose ground-plane bounds are farther than this from the player are disabled and leave the physics space; re-evaluated at each viewpoint; 0 = all off, which needs `player_physics: false`), `vsync_mode` (`disabled`/`enabled`/`adaptive`/`mailbox`), `vehicle_sleep` (false keeps parked vehicles awake, Phase 4b A/B; applied to a copy of each vehicle's definition), `npcs` (false takes the NPCs out of the simulation), `npc_far_mode` (false keeps every NPC under the controller; Phase 5 A/B), and for a map that implements `apply_runtime_settings(runtime, camera)` (the shader facade map; 2026-11-01): `facade_material` (`shader`, `flat`: the GLB material on the panels, i.e. the wall colour, `simple`: `facade_windows_simple.gdshader` without filtering, mullion, or distant fade), `facade_priority` (the window shader's render priority), `facade_warmup` (one facade cell drawn at 1 cm in front of the camera for three frames after loading); read when a map loads (Phase 6c): `streaming` (false loads a streamed map whole) and `streaming_index` (another index, e.g. another cell size); read at the start (Phase 6e): `threaded_load` (false loads every map at once on the main thread, as before Phase 6e: the "before" of the loading screen measurements). Keys an experiment leaves out keep their defaults; the result's `config.runtime` records them.
- **Applying:** `game.apply_perf_experiment()` merges the experiment over its profile and applies it through the same `RenderSettings`; there is no separate experiment code path.
- **Categories** (the game's vocabulary `CategoryRules.CATEGORIES`; since 2026-11-02 each map's `MapDefinition.category_rules` maps its meshes onto them, see "Map categories"; for the İstanbul maps by node-name prefix in this order, `maps/istanbul_halic/istanbul_halic_categories.tres`; every one of the 3,055 map meshes matches one, enforced by `test_perf_categories_cover_every_map_mesh`, and every one of the cells map's 6,129, enforced by `test_every_mesh_has_a_category_and_cells_are_named`):

- **Loading:** `game.gd` reads `--perf-config=<path>` from the command line (desktop) or a one-shot `perf_bench.json` (devices; deleted when read) from `user://`, or on Android from the app-specific external folder `OS.get_system_dir(SYSTEM_DIR_DOCUMENTS, false)` (`/sdcard/Android/data/<package>/files/Documents`): a release APK is not debuggable, so `run-as` cannot reach `user://`, but adb reaches that folder directly. Experiment definitions live in `tools/perf/experiments/*.json`.
- **Benchmark:** `PerfBenchmark` visits each viewpoint, waits `warmup_s`, then records every frame time for `measure_s`: average FPS (frames / time), 1 % low (FPS at the 99th-percentile frame time), minimum FPS (longest frame), frame-time percentiles (`frame_p50_ms`, `frame_p90_ms`, `frame_p99_ms`), a 1 ms histogram (`frame_hist_ms`, 0–99 ms plus one bin for longer frames), `long_frames` (over 1.25 × the median, i.e. at least one display refresh late), the physics step and process times (`physics_ms`/`physics_p99_ms`, `process_ms`/`process_p99_ms`: `Performance.TIME_PHYSICS_PROCESS` and `TIME_PROCESS` sampled every frame), and per-second averages of draw calls, primitives, and objects, plus peak video memory and engine static memory, and `vehicles_asleep` (share of frames in which the map's vehicles slept). Each result records the active profile and `in_vehicle`; the results file also has `startup` with the build kind and frame/physics settings (`debug_build`, `perf_feature`, `physics_ticks`, `physics_interpolation`, `max_fps`, `vsync_mode`, `screen_refresh_rate` (panel maximum), `display_refresh_rate` (the rate frames are paced to), `ios_allow_high_refresh_rate`, `android_frame_pacing`) and the vehicles' sleep state at the first frame (`vehicles`), the map (`map`), and the number of NPCs (`npcs`); each result also records `npcs` (NPCs simulated) and `npcs_near` (average number under the controller rather than in the far mode). It logs `BENCH {json}` lines, writes `user://bench_results.json` (with the profile, the occluder source and load time, and the startup times), saves 640 px JPEG screenshots to `user://bench/`, then quits (Android) or hides the map and drops to 5 FPS until the host terminates it (iOS ignores `quit()`).
- **Streaming in results** (Phase 6c): on a streamed map each result has `streaming` (cells loaded at the end, their MB, loads, unloads, and primes during the window, frames with attached cells, the longest attaching time in a frame, and on a route the frames with a needed cell missing); a trace also lists `cells` ([frame, cells, ms] per frame with attached cells) and, since Phase 6d, `requested` and `freed` ([frame, cells] per frame in which cell loads were started or cells were freed; `MapStreamer.frame_requested`, `frame_detached`). `python3 -I tools/perf/streaming_summary.py RESULTS.json…` tabulates them with the frames over 50 ms and how many fall within one frame of an attach, a request, or a free; `--timeline [--over MS]` lists each such frame instead, with its loop, GPU, and render CPU times, the nearest attach, request, and free (frames away), and the pipelines compiled next to it.
- **Route benchmark** (Phase 6c; `"bench": {"route": [[x, y, z], …], "route_speed": m/s, "route_back": true, "in_vehicle": …}`): instead of viewpoints, the player (or the vehicle with the player in it, frozen kinematic) is moved by the benchmark along the route every physics tick at the given speed, facing along it, out and back, one result per direction (`Route out`, `Route back`), measured from start to end; no input, no collision, so every run takes the same path. `tools/perf/make_route.gd -- --map=<id> --via=x,z;x,z;…` makes a route over walkable ground (A* on a 2 m grid over ground colliders, then straightened). Experiments: `c_route_walk_shader` (4.5 m/s), `c_route_drive_shader` (15 m/s in the car), and their `_off` variants (streaming off), the default map's 515 m route from the Galata Bridge over Karaköy up to the Galata street.
- **Traced benchmark** (`"bench": {"trace": true}`, 2026-11-01): the warm-up is sampled too (`warmup_long_frames`, `warmup_pipelines`), each result gets the GPU and render CPU time per frame (`gpu_ms`/`gpu_p99_ms`, `render_cpu_ms`/`render_cpu_p99_ms`, from `RenderingServer.viewport_set_measure_render_time`; 0 on Metal; on the Galaxy Tab A7 it changes only every few seconds and stays near 25 ms on every map and facade variant, as the driver scales the GPU clock, so it is no measure of load there), the pipelines compiled per source (`pipelines`: canvas, mesh, surface, draw, specialization), and `trace` with every frame's times as comma-separated milliseconds (`frame_ms`, `gpu_ms`, `render_cpu_ms`) and the frames at which pipelines were compiled. `BENCH` log lines leave the trace out. `python3 -I tools/perf/trace_summary.py RUN_DIR...` shows when the long frames fall (per 10 s, first and last, gaps, streaks, how many sit next to a short frame, i.e. pacing jitter).

### Device scenario (Phase 4d)

An experiment with a `scenario` section (`name`: `vehicle_sleep`, `orientation`, or `extensibility`; `rotate_hold_s`; `quit_when_done`) makes a debug build play `DeviceScenario` instead of a benchmark: synthetic touches through `Input.parse_input_event` (the path real touches take) on the debug CAR and VIEW buttons, the joystick, and the interact, throttle, handbrake, and exit buttons, with one `SCENARIO step=<name> result=<PASS|FAIL|INFO> t=<s> <details>` log line per step. Results go to the benchmark's results file, so the host scripts collect them.

- Every position comes from the loaded map's `MapDefinition` (since 2026-11-02, Phase 6a), so the scenarios run on any map, imported ones included: the first vehicle, the NPCs and their routes, the viewpoints, and `sloped_viewpoint` (the index of a viewpoint on sloped ground with room for a car; 1, Galata, on the İstanbul maps; found by the import tool, -1 when there is none).
- `vehicle_sleep`: parked car asleep → CAR → walk into the car (wakes) → let go (asleep within 4 s, and asleep for 3 s more while the player stands against it) → get in (wakes) → throttle above 5 m/s → handbrake, get out → asleep; then the same on the map's sloped viewpoint (VIEW while driving; `slope_skipped` INFO on a map without one). With `rotate_hold_s` > 0 it continues with `orientation`.
- `extensibility` (Phase 5): MAP to the test district (`map_switch`: spawn, its vehicles and NPCs as its definition lists them, node count; with a `maps` key the first map listed is visited instead, switched to and back with `Main.switch_map()` rather than through every map in between) → CAR until beside the van, get in, throttle above 5 m/s, handbrake, get out, asleep again (`van_*`) → the nearest NPC on the player's level patrols (`npc_patrols`), the stick walks the player up to it and the punch button hits it (`npc_hit_reaction`, `npc_one_hit_per_punch`), it walks on (`npc_patrol_resumes`) → MAP back to the first map (`map_back`: spawn, its vehicles and NPCs, the node count equal to the count before the switches) → the `vehicle_sleep` steps.
- `orientation`: holds the joystick for `rotate_hold_s` while the device is turned, logging the gravity vector (the gravity sensor is enabled for debug builds only: `input_devices/sensors/enable_gravity.debug=true`), the applied safe area, and the stick once a second; then releases and checks that the stick reads zero and the player stops.
- `map_switch` (2026-11-01; keys `maps`, a list of registry ids, and `hold_s`, default 20): holds the first map, then switches straight to each listed map (`Main.switch_map()`, not the MAP button) and holds it; one `switch_<n>` step per switch (the map loaded, the player at its spawn, on a map seen before the node count it had then), with unix times in the details. `tools/perf/pss_trace.py sample <dir>` beside `android_bench.sh` (with `SAMPLE_PSS=0`) reads `dumpsys meminfo` about every 0.6 s until the app quits and saves the low-memory killer's log lines; `pss_trace.py summary <dir> <results.json>` gives each hold's stable PSS and each switch's peak.
- Experiments: `scenario_vehicle_sleep.json`, `scenario_vehicle_sleep_rotate.json` (12 s hold), `scenario_orientation.json` (30 s), `scenario_extensibility.json`, `scenario_vehicle_sleep_kenney_town.json` and `scenario_extensibility_kenney_town.json` (on the imported CC0 town; the second visits the test district directly), `scenario_map_switch.json` (shader → İstanbul → cells → İstanbul → shader → cells → shader, 20 s each). Headless: `test_device_scenario_on_the_map`, `test_device_scenario_extensibility`, `test_device_scenario_map_switch` (test district), `test_device_scenario_vehicle_sleep` on each imported map (`imported_map_case.gd`), `test_device_scenario_extensibility_switching_directly` (Kenney town).

```bash

tools/perf/android_rotate_on_cue.sh build/perf/scenario &
tools/perf/android_bench.sh tools/perf/experiments/scenario_vehicle_sleep_rotate.json build/perf/scenario
```

### Measurement protocol and host scripts

```bash
# Android (debug APK installed; do not run Godot --import/--export meanwhile: the editor stops adb on exit)
tools/perf/run_batch.sh android build/perf/android tools/perf/experiments/baseline.json ...
# iOS (debug app installed; Auto-Lock off)
IOS_UDID=<udid> tools/perf/run_batch.sh ios build/perf/ios tools/perf/experiments/baseline.json ...
python3 -I tools/perf/summarize.py build/perf/android   # Markdown tables
# From Phase 3 on: at least three runs per device and viewpoint, one output folder per run
for run in run1 run2 run3; do tools/perf/run_batch.sh android build/perf/<task>/android/$run tools/perf/experiments/profile_default.json ...; done
python3 -I tools/perf/median.py build/perf/<task>/android/run1 build/perf/<task>/android/run2 build/perf/<task>/android/run3
```

- **Presented frames on Android (2026-11-02):** `python3 -I tools/perf/present_trace.py sample <out> <experiment>` beside `android_bench.sh` records what SurfaceFlinger presented from the game's surface (`dumpsys SurfaceFlinger --latency` every 2 s, TimeStats, the engine log with CLOCK_MONOTONIC); `present_trace.py summary <run dirs>` compares the game's frame times with the presented intervals per viewpoint. The benchmark's long frames are the game loop's; on the Galaxy Tab A7 they are not what reaches the screen (`docs/perf/default_map/`). A poll can delay one composition, so the summary also counts the intervals away from polls.
- **One map against another (2026-11-02):** `python3 -I tools/perf/relative_check.py <candidate> <reference> <run dirs>` gives D25's relative criterion (bad launches, median 1 % low and average per viewpoint) from alternating launches.
- **Which map a plain launch starts on:** `tools/perf/android_startup_log.sh <out>` (removes any leftover experiment, launches, keeps the `STARTUP` / `QUALITY` / warning lines) and `tools/perf/ios_startup_log.sh <udid> <out>` (launches under an Instruments "Logging" recording and extracts the same lines, and `STREAMING`, from the os_log table; this reaches the engine's log lines on iOS, which the earlier captures did not; a normal release build prints `QUALITY` and `STREAMING` only).
- **Repeats (from 2026-10-25):** at least three runs per device, experiment, and viewpoint; report the median, with every run's value next to it (`tools/perf/median.py`). Identical single runs differed by 2–3 FPS in the tablet's 1 % low.
- Results also record the physics step time (`physics_ms`, `physics_p99_ms`: `Performance.TIME_PHYSICS_PROCESS` sampled every frame; it reports one 60 Hz step, so at a 30 FPS cap a frame contains two) and whether the run was in a vehicle (`in_vehicle`; `profile_default_vehicle`, `session_profile_vehicle`: the player sits in the map's first vehicle, moved to each viewpoint, no pedal pressed).

- Cold start for every run. Android: the batch waits until the battery is back to its starting temperature (+0.5 °C) and keeps the screen on while plugged in (`svc power stayon usb`, restored afterwards). iOS: each run waits for the Instruments thermal state "Nominal".
- Unlock both devices before a batch. The Android script only wakes the screen; behind a PIN lock screen the app never comes to the foreground and the run times out (40 min) without results. iOS needs Auto-Lock off: a locked iPhone makes every Instruments probe answer "Waiting for device to boot". `ios_bench.sh` first queries the device with `devicectl` (that restored the connection when it showed "available (paired)"), stops after three probes without a thermal state, and tolerates a single probe or process-list dropout.
- Per viewpoint (Spawn, Karaköy, Galata): 30 s warm-up, 60 s measurement. The app is closed between runs.
- Recorded per run: Android battery temperature and thermal status at start and end, the battery's level, charging status, charge current (mA, negative while it drains), and power source at start and end (`battery_start`, `battery_end`; Phase 6d), the cool-down wait before the run (`cool_wait_s`, target `cool_target_c`), peak PSS (`dumpsys meminfo` every 15 s); iOS thermal state and app physical footprint from 3 s "Activity Monitor" probes every ~45 s (`tools/perf/parse_xctrace.py`). A run that reaches "Serious" on iOS is invalid.
- The probes themselves add load. On the tablet, `dumpsys meminfo` every 15 s causes single 100+ ms frames, which lowers the minimum FPS (average unaffected); set `SAMPLE_PSS=0` for frame-pacing measurements. On iOS an Instruments session runs for 3 s of every ~45 s.
- Results, conditions, and screenshots of the 2026-10-24 pass are kept in `docs/perf/` (`results/<device>/<experiment>.json` and `.meta.json`; 480 px screenshots); the profile validation in `docs/perf/profiles/`, the Phase 2 regression in `docs/perf/phase2/`. Regenerate the tables with `python3 -I tools/perf/summarize.py docs/perf/results/android --compact` (or the other `results/<device>` folders).
- Renderer comparison: Android uses the "Android Debug Compatibility" preset (`--rendering-method gl_compatibility` embedded); iOS passes the same argument at launch (`APP_ARGS`).

- **Release registry:** `game/world/maps_release.tres` (`MapRegistry.RELEASE_PATH`) lists the maps a release package carries: today only the default map, the first of the full registry. `main.tscn` names no registry; `Main._ready()` takes `MapRegistry.for_build()`: the full registry (`maps.tres`) in debug and Release Perf builds, the release registry in normal release builds, which never choose a map anyway (T34). So a release package needs neither `maps.tres` nor the other maps' definitions; no code names a map.
- **The exclude filter is written, not typed** (Phase 6e, T60): `godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/release_package.gd -- --write` writes both release presets' `exclude_filter` in `export_presets.cfg` from the release registry (`exclude_patterns()`; `--check` exits 1 when it is not current), and `tools/build_release.sh` runs it before every export, so a map that is not in the release registry is left out whole without anyone naming it. The filter: `tests/*`, `tools/*`, `docs/*`, `maps/*/streaming_*`, the full registry, then everything under `maps/`, `assets/`, and `scripts/` and every scene at the top of the project that the release maps do not need (`needed()`), a whole folder where none of it is needed, and the streaming folder of every map of the full registry that the release leaves out (generated per machine, so it is named even where it is not written). A model's `.import` file stands for the model, so the same tracked files give the same filter on every machine. `check_release.gd` stops the export when the preset's filter differs from the written one.
- **Adding a map to the release:** add its definition to `game/world/maps_release.tres`, write its cells on the exporting machine (streamed maps: `tools/cell_map.gd -- --map=<id>`, then `--import`), run `tools/release_package.gd -- --write` (or let `tools/build_release.sh` do it), commit `export_presets.cfg` with the registry, and build; `test_release_package.gd` fails until the presets carry the written filter.
- **What the presets pack** (`exclude_filter`, the same in both, written as above): everything but `tests/`, `tools/`, `docs/`, every GLB and the map metadata (`assets/*`), the original explorer demo (`main.tscn`, `scripts/explorer.gd`, `scripts/main.gd`), the full registry, every comparison split (`maps/*/streaming_*`), and every map folder or file the release maps do not use. What stays of `maps/`: the default map's definition, script, shaders, and streaming folder (resident scene, 2,612 cells, 7 materials), İstanbul's map script and category rules (the default map's script extends that script), the cells map's occluders, and `scripts/water.gdshader` (preloaded by İstanbul's script). The default map's own scene (`istanbul_halic_cells_shader.tscn`, which instances the GLB) and its GLB stay out: the package carries the map streamed only, which `MapDefinition.is_available()` accepts ("Maps and the map registry").
- **Check before export:** `tools/build_release.sh` writes the exclude filter, then runs `tools/check_release.gd -- "<preset>"`, which stops the export (exit 1) when the preset's filter is not the written one, when the simulated package differs from what the release maps need (below) or when a release map cannot go into it as it is on this machine (`release_package.gd`, `cells_problem()`): no cells written, a cell, material, or the resident scene missing, the cells stale against the GLB's import, or no GLB to compare them with (`MapStreamingIndex.problem(true)`); the message names `tools/cell_map.gd -- --map=<id>` and the import. It writes the list of project files the package should hold (`build/release/<platform>.expected.txt`).
- **Listing the package:** after the export, `tools/release_contents.py <apk|pck> --expect <list>` reads the APK (`assets/`) or the iOS `.pck` (formats 2–4), maps every packed file back to its project file (`X.remap`, `X.import`, and `X.gdc` stand for `X`; every `.godot/exported/` and `.godot/imported/` file must be what a packed remap or import names), prints the sizes per folder, and fails on an unexpected, missing, or orphaned file. Godot's export itself is not changed by any of this; the presets' filters decide what it packs.
- **Tests:** `tests/unit/test_release_package.gd`: the registry a build loads, the release registry starting on the default map, both presets leaving out every other map of the full registry (definition, cells, models) and the models of the release maps, no custom features, empty keystore fields, the D8 identifiers, and the simulated package of both presets (`tools/release_package.gd`: Godot's "all resources" export of every resource file outside hidden and `.gdignore` folders, minus the exclude filter, matched as Godot matches it) holding exactly what the release maps need: their definitions and what those depend on, the scripts and shaders their scripts extend or preload, their occluders, and their streaming folders, and nothing else under `maps/`, `assets/`, or `scripts/` and no scene at the top; the presets carrying the filter written from the release registry, and a new map folder (a fake map under `maps/`, added to a copy of the full registry with a streaming index not written) left out whole by the written filter with the package unchanged (Phase 6e); and the export check on a synthetic map (no cells, current cells, a changed model, no model). `test_export_presets.gd` and `test_build_features.gd` check every preset as before.

## Conventions

- One `class_name` per reusable module; `##` doc comments at the top of each script; tabs for indentation.
- Scenes are `.tscn` text files; shared tunables live in resources (`CameraProfile`, `MapDefinition`).
- Commit the `.uid` files Godot generates next to scripts.
- Exported script properties use `Array[String]`, not `PackedStringArray`: Godot 4.7.2's export conversion of `.tres` files dropped an exported `PackedStringArray`. `test_no_packed_string_array_exports` enforces this.
